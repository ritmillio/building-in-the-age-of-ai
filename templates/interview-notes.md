# Customer interview notes (template)

> One file per conversation, filled in within an hour of it. Five of these are what the synthesis prompt in [succeed.md](../guides/succeed.md#1-validate-before-you-build) needs: real words, real past events, real costs. Write what they said, not what you heard.

```markdown
# Interview: [role], [company type and size], [date]

How we met: [intro / cold / existing customer]   Recorded: [yes, with consent / no]

## Their words (verbatim, in quotes)
- "..."
- "..."

## The last time it happened
- When: [date or "last week"]
- What they did, step by step: [...]
- What it cost: [hours, money, a missed deadline, a customer lost]
- Who else was involved: [...]

## What they use today instead
- [tool, spreadsheet, person, nothing] and what they pay for it: [money or hours]
- What they tried before and dropped, and why: [...]

## Signals
- Commitment they offered (a next meeting, an intro, a pilot, money): [...]
- Compliments and "I would use that": [write them here so they do NOT count as evidence]

## What I still don't know
- [...]
```

## Rules for filling it in

- **Past, not future.** "The last time it happened" is the heart of the note. If you have nothing there, the interview was about your idea, not their life ([The Mom Test](https://www.momtestbook.com/)).
- **Quote, don't paraphrase.** The synthesis prompt can only quote what you wrote down. Your summary of their words is your opinion.
- **Commitment beats compliments.** A next meeting, an intro or a pilot is evidence. "Great idea" is not, which is why it has its own line.
- **No names in shared copies.** If the notes go into a shared folder or an AI tool your company has not approved, replace names and companies with a role ("ops lead, 40-person logistics firm").

## Then

Paste five notes into the synthesis prompt in [succeed.md](../guides/succeed.md#1-validate-before-you-build) (the one that starts "Role: a skeptical researcher"). It ranks the problems by how often they came up and what they cost, keeps the quotes, and marks what you wanted to hear but nobody said.
